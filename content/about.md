*Provenance* publishes critical reflections, practical tutorials, reusable templates, and short field notes about generative AI in historical research and teaching.

We start from the historian's habits of mind: ask where a source came from, who made it, and why. We apply those questions to AI tools and to what they produce. We are neither boosters nor abolitionists. Our goal is to help historians make informed decisions about using these tools, and about declining to use them.

## Who we are

*Provenance* is edited at the [Roy Rosenzweig Center for History and New Media](https://rrchnm.org/) at George Mason University. It is a companion to, and separate from, [*Current Research in Digital History*](https://crdh.rrchnm.org/), the center's peer-reviewed journal.

## What we publish

- **Essays:** arguments and reflections on what AI changes, and what it doesn't.
- **Tutorials:** reproducible workflows with verification built in.
- **Templates:** prompts, policies, and syllabus language you can adapt.
- **Field Notes:** short reports from experiments, classrooms, and archives.
