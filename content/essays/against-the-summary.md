The pitch is seductive: upload three hundred letters and get back a tidy summary of their themes. For a historian with a heavy teaching load and a deadline, it sounds like a gift. ## The tyranny of the typical

Summaries favor the typical. Archives reward the strange. The letter that breaks a pattern, the ledger entry in a different hand, the complaint written in the margin: these are exactly what a summary smooths away.

{{< pullquote >}}A summary tells you what a source is mostly about. Historians usually need to know what it is *unexpectedly* about.{{< /pullquote >}}

## A narrower use

None of this means summaries are useless. They can work as a triage tool, a way to decide which folders to read closely first. The danger comes when the triage becomes the reading.

A good rule of thumb: if you would not cite the summary, do not let it decide what you never read.
