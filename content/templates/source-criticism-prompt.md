Historians ask the same questions of every source: who made it, when, for whom, and why? This template asks a model to apply those questions to its own answer. That won't make the answer reliable, but it often surfaces where the answer is weakest.{{< sidenote >}}Template text is placeholder content for the prototype.{{< /sidenote >}}

{{< prompt title="Source criticism of an AI answer" >}}
Review your previous answer as a historian would review a primary source.

1. Origin: Which specific sources, if any, could support each claim? Say plainly when you cannot name one.
2. Certainty: Label each claim as well established, contested, or uncertain.
3. Silences: Whose perspectives or which kinds of evidence are missing from the answer?
4. Anachronism: Do any terms or framings project present-day assumptions onto the past?
5. Verification: List three things a researcher should check in an archive or catalog before relying on this answer.
{{< /prompt >}}

## How to use it

- Run it right after a model answers a historical question.
- In class, have students compare the model's self-critique with their own critique.
- Treat the output as a checklist, not a verdict. A model saying "this is well established" proves nothing.
