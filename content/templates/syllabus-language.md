Generic AI policies usually focus on plagiarism. History courses also need to address evidence, citation, and the difference between a source and a summary. Pick the version that fits your course, and edit it freely.{{< sidenote >}}Placeholder policy language for the prototype.{{< /sidenote >}}

## Restrictive

{{< prompt title="Policy: No generative AI" >}}
In this course you are learning to find, read, and interpret historical sources yourself. Do not use generative AI tools to draft, summarize, or translate any work you submit. If you are unsure whether a tool counts, ask me first.
{{< /prompt >}}

## Guided

{{< prompt title="Policy: Disclosed use" >}}
You may use generative AI tools for brainstorming and for checking grammar. You may not use them to locate or summarize sources. Any use must be described in a short note at the end of your assignment: which tool, what you asked, and how you used the result.
{{< /prompt >}}

## Open, with criticism

{{< prompt title="Policy: AI as a source" >}}
You may use generative AI tools, but treat everything they produce as a source that needs criticism. Cite the tool and date, keep a log of your prompts, and verify every factual claim against a primary or scholarly source before including it.
{{< /prompt >}}
